# Structured Analytic Techniques Primer

This reference distills the 2009 US Government tradecraft primer mirrored at `../source-docs/us-government-structured-analytic-techniques-primer-2009.md` and the CIS CTI public guide on SAT usage.

## Why Use SATs
- To challenge hidden assumptions and mental models.
- To keep contradictory or ambiguous evidence visible.
- To surface alternatives before a preferred story hardens.
- To give the consumer a reasoning path that can be audited or updated.

## Technique Families

### Diagnostic
- Key assumptions check
- Quality of information check
- Indicators or signposts of change
- Analysis of competing hypotheses

### Contrarian
- Devil's advocacy
- Team A / Team B
- High-impact / low-probability analysis
- "What if?" analysis

### Imaginative
- Brainstorming
- Outside-in thinking
- Red-team or red-hat analysis
- Alternative futures analysis

## Selection Cues
- Use **diagnostic** techniques when the main need is to clarify the evidence, assumptions, or logic already in play.
- Use **contrarian** techniques when the lead explanation is getting too comfortable or institutional pressure may be distorting the draft.
- Use **imaginative** techniques when the space of possible outcomes is wider than the current evidence record.

## Practical Combinations
- Key assumptions check -> source evaluation -> intelligence analysis
- ACH -> estimative probability -> defend analytic conclusion
- Team A / Team B -> defend analytic conclusion -> intelligence writing
- Alternative futures -> indicators/signposts -> intelligence writing

## Output Habit
Whatever technique you choose, preserve three things:
- what question the technique is answering
- what evidence or assumptions drove the result
- what indicators would change the current ranking or scenario
