# Confidence Language

Use disciplined estimative language and keep probability separate from analytic confidence.

## Probability Terms
These bands are adapted from CIS CTI's published guidance and are broad enough to stay useful without encouraging false precision.

| Term | Approximate Range | Use When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Almost certainly not | 1-10% | The event remains possible but the evidence and base rates strongly weigh against it. |
| Very unlikely | 10-25% | The event is meaningfully less likely than not. |
| Unlikely | 25-40% | The event is plausible but not the lead expectation. |
| Roughly even chance | 45-55% | The evidence does not yet justify leaning strongly either way. |
| Likely | 60-75% | The event is the lead expectation, but live alternatives remain. |
| Very likely | 75-90% | The event is strongly favored by the evidence and current drivers. |
| Almost certain | 90-99% | The event would require a meaningful surprise or reversal not to occur. |

## Analytic Confidence Levels
- **High confidence:** Multiple, high-quality, mutually reinforcing sources support the judgment; major contradictions are limited or resolved.
- **Moderate confidence:** The judgment is plausible and reasonably supported, but source quality, corroboration, disagreement, or volatility prevents a stronger call.
- **Low confidence:** The reporting is thin, fragmented, questionable, stale, or too weakly corroborated to support strong inference.

## Rules
- State the proposition first, then choose the probability term.
- Explain why the confidence is at that level; do not let the label float by itself.
- Lower confidence when evidence is thin, stale, contradictory, heavily inferred, or potentially deceptive.
- Use numeric ranges only when the audience benefits from them.
- Pair the estimate with indicators that would move it up or down.

## Common Mistakes
- Treating probability and confidence as the same concept.
- Using stronger words because they sound more decisive.
- Mixing several probability vocabularies in the same product.
- Omitting the reason the estimate is not higher or lower.

## Sample Formula
`We assess with [confidence] that [proposition] is [estimative term] because [two or three strongest drivers]. This would change if [indicator or contrary evidence].`
