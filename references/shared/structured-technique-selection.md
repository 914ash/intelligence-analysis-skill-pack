# Structured Technique Selection

Choose the lightest technique that still makes the reasoning auditable. This guide combines the 2009 SAT primer's diagnostic, contrarian, and imaginative categories with the practical selection cues used in CIS CTI guidance.

## Start Here
- Use a lightweight core flow when the question is narrow, there are few serious alternatives, and the consumer mainly needs a quick judgment with clear caveats.
- Move into a structured technique when the cost of being wrong is high, the evidence is ambiguous, or the main risk is hidden bias rather than missing prose polish.

## Quick Selection Table

| Situation | Best First Technique | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The framing or assumptions feel shaky | Key assumptions check or `critical-thinking` | Surface hidden premises before the analysis hardens. |
| Several explanations remain plausible | ACH or `alternative-analysis` | Force a visible comparison rather than quiet preference. |
| The lead view is institutionally favored | Team A / Team B or Devil's Advocacy | Create a structured challenge to consensus. |
| Evidence is sparse but action may still be required | TRACE | Keep conditional reasoning visible instead of faking certainty. |
| The issue is networked, relational, or intermediary-heavy | Node-link analysis | Structure entities and links before narrative synthesis. |
| The future space is broad and highly uncertain | Alternative futures or indicators/signposts | Explore range, then monitor what future is emerging. |
| The answer depends on several expert perspectives | Delphi | Capture disagreement and convergence without dominance effects. |

## Technique Families
- **Diagnostic:** Key assumptions check, quality of information check, indicators/signposts, ACH.
- **Contrarian:** Devil's advocacy, Team A / Team B, high-impact / low-probability analysis, "What if?" analysis.
- **Imaginative:** Brainstorming, outside-in thinking, red-team or red-hat analysis, alternative futures.

## Combination Patterns
- `craft-intelligence-question` -> `research-and-collection` -> `source-evaluation` -> `intelligence-analysis`
- `source-evaluation` -> `analysis-of-competing-hypotheses` -> `defend-analytic-conclusion`
- `research-and-collection` -> `node-link-analysis` -> `intelligence-analysis`
- `critical-thinking` -> `alternative-analysis` -> `estimative-probability`

## Escalation Rule
If two techniques seem equally suitable, prefer the one that exposes disconfirming evidence, weak assumptions, or decision-relevant uncertainty more clearly.
