# Writing Patterns

This note is informed by the concise, implications-first habits seen in outlets such as The Cipher Brief and Semafor Flagship, plus The Intelligence Shop's tradecraft-oriented writing examples.

## Core Style Signals
- Lead with the assessment in the first sentence or first short paragraph.
- Keep the implication close to the judgment instead of burying it several sections later.
- Use concrete nouns and verbs; avoid generic management language.
- Let headers carry meaning: readers should understand the logic just by scanning the structure.
- Keep paragraphs short enough to scan quickly, but long enough to make a complete analytic point.

## Recommended Section Order
1. Title that states the subject and angle.
2. BLUF or bottom line.
3. Key judgments or top findings.
4. Analytic body ordered from most important to least important.
5. Alternatives, gaps, watch items, or method note as needed.

## Good Writing Moves
- Distinguish clearly between reporting and assessment.
- Use one paragraph to carry one main analytic idea.
- Put the strongest evidence close to the claim it supports.
- Show the consumer why the point matters now.
- End with what would change the assessment or what to monitor next.

## Editing Checks
- Can the first three lines stand alone?
- Does every major paragraph advance the argument rather than repeat the topic?
- Are confidence and caveats visible where they matter most?
- Could a hurried reader extract the main answer from the title, BLUF, and key judgments alone?

## Avoid
- Teaser openings that delay the answer.
- Hype, cleverness, or thriller prose.
- Abstract phrases such as "in today's rapidly evolving landscape."
- Implication language that is broader than the actual evidence base.
